Conclusion

Estimated time: 5 minutes

Review the contribution and limits of each data source, then apply the workflow to a new candidate list.

What you accomplished

The workflow retained all 54 published variant rows while adding bulk-tissue expression from GTEx, cell-type expression from HuBMAP, target information from Pharos, and protein predictions from ProtVar. Each result remained traceable to its source, identifiers, and retrieval date.

From evidence to the next action

GTEx can guide tissue selection, HuBMAP can guide cell-model selection, Pharos can identify existing target knowledge or tools, and ProtVar can guide protein-focused follow-up.

For the TNNT2 VUS, the results motivate experiments examining troponin-complex function, calcium sensitivity, or cardiomyocyte contractility.

Before applying these findings clinically, an analyst would also evaluate phenotype concordance, segregation, population frequency, and appropriate functional evidence.

A reusable prioritization checklist

  • Name the exact variant and the source of its annotation.
  • Ask what biological level each dataset measures.
  • Keep missing data separate from a measured zero.
  • Save dataset versions, identifiers, and retrieval dates.
  • Keep druggability separate from disease relevance.
  • End with a clear next step that can be tested.

Suggested next steps and reading

Use these papers to explore specific next steps:

  • Variant prioritization: Stenton et al. (2024), co-authored by Elizabeth A. Worthey, compares rare-disease variant prioritization methods and the evidence used to rank candidates.
  • Protein structure: Varga et al. (2025) explains how protein structures can inform variant interpretation and how the choice of structural model affects the analysis.
  • Splicing and RNA evidence: Truty et al. (2021) examines how RNA analysis can help interpret variants predicted to affect splicing.
  • Clinical WGS workflow: Worthey et al. (2012) describes an early clinical whole-genome sequencing workflow that considered variant quality, functional effect, disease knowledge, and phenotype relevance together.
